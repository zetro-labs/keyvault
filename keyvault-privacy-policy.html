3Your vault data

Everything you save in KeyVault — entries, usernames, passwords, website addresses, notes, attached files, passcodes, two-factor (2FA/TOTP) secrets, favourites, and passkey records — is stored only on your device, inside an encrypted vault:

If you use multi-device sync, the names of your synced devices and a short sync activity log are kept inside the same encrypted vault. So Android can offer your passkeys at sign-in, a small index of passkey site and account names is also kept on the device; it holds no keys or passwords and never leaves your phone.

  • Data is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, an authenticated encryption standard, before it is written to storage.
  • The encryption key is derived from your master password using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations, which makes brute-force guessing extremely expensive.
  • Your master password never leaves your device and is never known to us. It is not stored in plain text.
  • When the app is locked or sent to the background, decrypted vault data is cleared from memory. The app always opens on the lock screen first.
  • Notes and attachments. Passwords and passcodes can carry notes and attached files (any file type, up to 10 MB each), and a saved passkey can carry a note and files added from its detail view. Attachments are stored only inside the encrypted vault on your device — and, if you enable backup or sync, inside your own encrypted backups. They are never uploaded anywhere by Zetro Labs.

The honest trade-off of zero-knowledge design: because we never see your master password, we cannot reset it or recover your vault for you. If you forget your master password and have not saved your Emergency Access recovery kit, your data cannot be recovered — by anyone. See the FAQ for how Emergency Access works.

4Biometric unlock

If you enable fingerprint unlock, the key that unwraps your vault is protected by the Android Keystore and released only after Android verifies your fingerprint. Your fingerprint data itself is handled entirely by Android and your device hardware — KeyVault never receives, sees, or stores your biometric data.

5Optional Google Drive backup

KeyVault can optionally back up your vault to your own Google Drive. This is off by default and happens only if you link your Google account and use the backup, restore, or sync features in Settings.

  • The backup is stored in Drive’s hidden app data folder using the drive.appdata permission scope. KeyVault can access only its own app folder — it cannot see, read, or modify any of your other Drive files.
  • The vault is encrypted on your device before anything is uploaded. What sits on Google Drive is an encrypted file that only your master password can open. Google cannot read it, and neither can we.
  • Zetro Labs has no access to your Google account or your Drive. Linking uses Google’s own sign-in flow, and you can unlink at any time in Settings.
  • You can delete the backup at any time by unlinking and removing the app data from your Google Drive settings.

Google’s handling of your account and Drive storage is governed by Google’s own privacy policy and terms.

6Breach checks (Have I Been Pwned)

The Security tab can check whether your passwords appear in known data breaches, using the Have I Been Pwned (HIBP) service and its k-anonymity model, designed so your passwords are never exposed:

  • Your password is hashed with SHA-1 on your device.
  • Only the first 5 characters of that hash leave your device. HIBP returns every known hash beginning with those 5 characters, and your device compares the full hash locally.
  • Your actual password and its full hash are never transmitted. HIBP cannot identify you or your password from a 5-character prefix — the same prefix is shared by enormous numbers of passwords worldwide.
  • Breach checks need an internet connection (see Section 8). HIBP returns match counts only; by design, it cannot say which company leaked a given password.

7Passkeys and Autofill

On Android 14 and later, KeyVault can act as a passkey provider and an Autofill service. Passkeys you create are stored inside your encrypted vault like everything else. When you sign in to a website or app, Android’s system picker handles the ceremony; the passkey is used on your device and only the cryptographic proof — never the private key — is shared with the site you are signing in to. KeyVault sends no information about your passkeys or sign-ins to Zetro Labs or anyone else. A saved passkey’s detail view also offers quick Add/Edit note and Attach file actions — no full edit needed; those notes and files are stored encrypted in your vault, tied to that passkey.

8Internet access

KeyVault has no servers, no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and your vault works fully offline. The app uses the internet only for features you choose to use: Google Drive backup, sync and restore; breach checks; opening websites from an item’s detail view; and fetching website icons (favicons) from Google’s favicon service when you open the Website picker — only the site’s domain name is involved in that icon lookup, never your data. Your vault contents leave the device only in encrypted form (Drive backups and sync), as a 5-character hash prefix (breach checks), or when you open a website yourself. If you set Breach checks to Automatic in Settings, a breach check also runs on its own when you unlock the app, at most once a day.

If you use Local sync, your phones connect directly to each other over your own Wi-Fi. Nothing passes through Google, Zetro Labs or any server. The same encrypted sync data travels phone-to-phone over a connection protected by a code you confirm on both phones.

9Permissions

KeyVault requests only the Android permissions its features genuinely need:

  • Internet — used only for the features listed in Section 8.
  • Biometric / fingerprint — only if you enable fingerprint unlock.
  • File access through Android’s system picker — when you export a backup or import a Chrome password CSV, you choose the file or folder yourself. KeyVault does not browse your storage. (Importing a backup needs no picker: it restores the most recent backup in your Download/KeyVault folder automatically.)
  • Installed apps (read locally only) — the Add Item “App” picker reads the names and icons of the apps installed on your phone, on the device only, so you can pick one to fill in an entry. Nothing about your installed apps is transmitted, and nothing is stored anywhere outside the entry you create.
  • Camera (on-device only, when you tap Scan) — KeyVault can use your camera for one purpose only: scanning a QR code when you tap Scan — a recovery-kit code on the recovery screen, or a two-factor (2FA/TOTP) setup code while adding or editing an entry. Scanning happens entirely on your device; no image is stored, transmitted, or seen by Zetro Labs, and the camera is never used at any other time.
  • Nearby devices (Android 13 and later) — used only to find and connect to your other phone for Local sync, when you start it. KeyVault never uses it to learn your location.

KeyVault does not request access to your contacts, location, microphone, call logs, or messages, and it never will for its core features.

10Data retention and deletion

  • Local backup files you exported (for example to your Download/KeyVault folder) are ordinary files under your control; delete them like any other file.

General

What is KeyVault?

KeyVault is a password manager for Android made by Zetro Labs. It stores your passwords, passkeys, and passcodes in a vault that is encrypted on your phone with a master password only you know. There is no account to create and no server involved — the app works fully offline.

Who created KeyVault, and who owns it?

KeyVault was created by Zetro Labs and is an exclusive product of Zetro Labs. Zetro Labs retains all rights and authority over the app. Any legal issues arising in connection with KeyVault are subject to the jurisdiction of the Indian judiciary (the courts of India).

Is KeyVault really free of accounts, ads, and tracking?

Yes. KeyVault has no sign-up, no sign-in, no advertisements, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. Zetro Labs receives nothing from the app — we don’t know who you are or what you store.

How is my vault encrypted?

Your data is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is derived from your master password using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations, which makes guessing attacks extremely slow and expensive. Your master password itself never leaves your device and is never stored in plain text.

Does KeyVault work without an internet connection?

Completely. Everything core — unlocking, viewing, adding, generating, settings — works with no connection at all. The internet is used only for features you choose to use — Google Drive backup/sync, breach checks, and opening websites — plus one small fetch: opening the Add Item website picker loads the listed sites’ favicons.

Can Zetro Labs see or recover my passwords?

No. This is by design (“zero-knowledge”): your data is encrypted with a key derived from your master password, and that password never leaves your phone. We cannot see your passwords, reset your master password, or unlock your vault — for anyone, under any circumstances.

Getting Started & Unlocking

How do I set up KeyVault for the first time?

Open the app and create a master password. Choose something long and memorable that you don’t use anywhere else — a phrase of several words works well. That single password protects everything in your vault. During setup you can also import an existing backup or your Chrome passwords if you have them.

What happens if I forget my master password?

Without your master password, your vault cannot be opened — and we cannot help, because we never have it. The one exception is Emergency Access: if you created a recovery kit beforehand (Settings → Security → Emergency access) and kept it safe, you can use it to get back in, after which you must set a new master password. If you have neither the password nor a recovery kit, the only option is to erase the vault and start over.

Can I unlock with my fingerprint instead of typing?

Yes. Enable fingerprint unlock in Settings → Security. Your vault key is then protected by the Android Keystore and released when Android verifies your fingerprint. Your fingerprint data never reaches KeyVault — Android handles it entirely. Turning fingerprint unlock on goes through Android’s own fingerprint enrollment.

Why does KeyVault ask for my password again after I’ve been away?

For your protection. KeyVault always opens on the lock screen first, and when the app goes to the background, decrypted vault data is cleared from memory. So whenever you return, you unlock again. That way, someone picking up your phone can’t walk into an open vault.

Do I have to re-authenticate to change my security settings?

No — changing security settings does not ask you to re-authenticate first. Turning fingerprint unlock on goes through Android’s own fingerprint enrollment. The one change that always asks for a password is changing your master password, which requires your current one.

Home Tab

What does the Home tab show?

Home is your at-a-glance dashboard: a search box, a security summary card, quick-add buttons, your Favourites, and your Recently used entries. It’s designed so the things you reach for most are one tap away.

What does the security card on Home mean?

It summarises how many of your passwords are weak or reused. Tap Review to open the Security tab, where you can see and fix each issue — including breach alerts.

What do the Quick-add buttons do?

New password and New passcode open the Add Item screen with that type already selected, so you can save a new entry in seconds.

What are Favourites, and how do I add one?

Favourites are the entries you pin for fast access — Home shows your first four. To favourite an entry, open it and mark it as a favourite. Tapping a favourite tile on Home opens that entry’s detail screen, just like tapping it in the Vault.

What is “Recently used”?

It lists the entries and passkeys you most recently added, changed, or used (the first three are shown on Home), so you can get back to them quickly. Tap See all to jump to the full Vault list.

What does the search box on Home do?

Typing a search on Home takes you to the Vault tab with that search applied, filtering your entries by title, username, or website address.

What does the lock button on Home do?

It locks KeyVault immediately and returns you to the lock screen — handy when handing your phone to someone else.

Vault Tab

What is the Vault tab?

The Vault tab is the complete list of everything you’ve saved — passwords, passkeys, and passcodes — with search, filters, and sorting. It’s where you manage your entries day to day. Passwords and passcodes are entries you add yourself in the app; passkeys cannot be added manually — they are created on websites and apps and registered into KeyVault when you choose KeyVault as the provider.

How does search work in the Vault?

Search filters the list as you type, matching against each entry’s title, username, and website address. Clear the search to see everything again.

What do the filter chips (All / Passwords / Passkeys / Passcodes) do?

They narrow the list to one kind of item: All shows everything, Passwords shows login entries, Passkeys shows passkeys, and Passcodes shows PINs and codes you’ve stored. Each chip shows how many items it contains.

How do I sort the Vault list?

Tap the sort button and a small menu appears next to it with three options: Name A–Z, Name Z–A, and Recently updated. Tap one to apply it — the current choice is marked with a checkmark, and KeyVault remembers your choice between sessions. Tap anywhere outside the menu to close it without changing anything.

Why do letter headings (A, B, C…) appear above some entries?

When you sort alphabetically (A–Z or Z–A), entries are grouped under their first letter to make scanning easier. Headings are hidden when sorting by “Recently updated”, since letters would be out of order there.

How do I view, copy, edit, or delete an entry?

Tap any row to open its detail screen. From there you can reveal or hide the password with the eye toggle, copy the username or password, edit the entry, or delete it. Passwords are hidden by default — reveal them only when you need to.

Note: passkeys can’t be edited (a passkey is a cryptographic key pair, not editable text), so the Edit button explains this instead of opening an editor. Passkeys can still be deleted.

How do I open the website saved with an entry?

In the entry’s detail view, the Website row has an open button that takes you straight to the site in your browser — opening a website needs an internet connection, so if your phone isn’t connected the app will say so. This works for passwords and passcodes with a website saved; for a passkey, the open button takes you to the passkey’s domain.

What is the Two-factor (2FA) secret field?

Many websites offer two-factor authentication (2FA): after your password, they ask for a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds (a TOTP code — the kind authenticator apps show). When a site shows you a setup QR code or setup key, save it in the entry’s Two-factor (2FA) secret field: tap Scan to scan the QR code, or type or paste the key — a pasted otpauth:// link works too. The secret is stored encrypted inside your vault, exactly like the password. KeyVault then shows the live code in the entry’s detail view and under the 2FA codes filter at the top of the Vault tab. Codes are computed on your phone and work fully offline; nothing about 2FA ever leaves your device.

Where do I see my 2FA codes?

Tap the 2FA codes filter at the top of the Vault tab. The list narrows to the entries that have a 2FA secret, and each row shows its current 6-digit code with the seconds left before it changes — the codes refresh on screen as you watch. Tap a row to copy its code; the clipboard clears automatically, just like copied passwords. An entry’s detail view shows its live code too. Because the codes are time-based, keep your phone’s date and time set to automatic — if the clock is off, the codes will not match what the website expects.

What is a passcode entry?

A passcode is a short code — like a PIN for a card, a locker code, or a door code — stored as its own entry type so it’s easy to find under the Passcodes filter.

Why don’t I see my saved passkey in the Vault?

Passkeys appear under both All and the Passkeys filter. If one seems missing, check that the Passkeys filter isn’t hiding other types you expected, and that you’re looking in the same vault you saved it to. Passkeys created through websites (via “Upgrade to passkey”) and passkeys created through Android’s system prompt are both stored in the vault and listed the same way.

Adding Items

How do I add a new item?

Tap the + button on the Vault tab, or a Quick-add button on Home. At the top of the Add Item screen, choose the type — Password or Passcode — then fill in the details.

What fields does a password entry have?

Two separate picker buttons — Website and App — sit above the Name field, and neither is selected until you tap one. Below them are the website address, the title (site or service name), username, and password, plus a Notes field and an Attach file button. Fill in as much as applies — the website address helps Autofill and the passkey-upgrade matching work better.

What do the Website and App buttons above the Name field do?

They are two separate buttons, and neither is selected by default — each is selected only when you tap it. Tapping Website opens a bottom sheet listing about 52 of the most-used websites worldwide, each with its favicon. Tapping App opens a bottom sheet listing every app installed on your phone, in alphabetical order, each with its real app icon. Both sheets have a search box. Tapping an entry fills in the Name and the Website URL automatically — you can still edit them afterwards.

Where do the icons in the Website and App lists come from?

Website icons are the sites’ own favicons, fetched from Google’s favicon service at the moment you open the website picker. The request asks only for the popular sites’ domain icons — no user data, vault data, or personal information is sent. App icons are read locally from your phone; nothing about your installed apps ever leaves your device. If an icon can’t load, a coloured letter tile is shown instead, so the list always looks complete.

Can I add notes and files to a password or passcode?

Yes. The Add and Edit forms for passwords and passcodes have a Notes field and an Attach file button. You can attach any file type, up to 10 MB each. Notes and files are stored encrypted inside your vault on your device — and inside your own encrypted backups or Drive sync if you have those enabled — and are never uploaded anywhere by Zetro Labs. In the entry’s detail view, attached files appear with an Open button.

What fields does a passcode entry have?

A name, the code itself, and a Website (optional) field just below the same Website/App picker buttons, plus a Notes field and an Attach file button. A passcode with a website saved gets the same open-website button in its detail view that passwords have.

What does the password strength meter tell me?

As you type or generate a password, the meter estimates how hard it would be to guess. Longer passwords with a mix of characters — or generated passwords and passphrases — score highest. Treat a low score as a prompt to use the built-in generator.

How do I add a passkey from the Add Item screen?

You don’t — passkeys cannot be added manually, and there is no Passkey option on the Add Item screen. A passkey is created on the website or app itself: when it offers “Create a passkey”, choose KeyVault as the provider and the passkey is registered into your vault. For that to work, KeyVault must be enabled under “Auto-fill passwords and passkeys” in your phone’s settings. To turn an existing password login into a passkey, see the Security tab’s “Upgrade to passkey”.

Generator Tab

What does the Generator do?

It creates strong, random credentials so you never have to invent passwords yourself. It has three modes — Password, Passphrase, and PIN — switchable at the top of the screen.

How does Password mode work?

Drag the slider to set the length (minimum 6 characters) and KeyVault generates a random password. Longer is stronger — 16 or more characters is a good default for important accounts.

How does Passphrase mode work?

Passphrase mode builds a password from random words, which is long, strong, and much easier to type and remember than random characters. Drag the slider to set the number of words (minimum 6).

How does PIN mode work?

PIN mode generates a random numeric code. Drag the slider to set the number of digits (minimum 4). Use it for passcode entries where a site or device only accepts digits.

What can I do with a generated password?

Tap to regenerate for a new one, copy it to the clipboard, or save it straight into your vault as a new entry. If you copy it, KeyVault clears the clipboard automatically after the time set in Settings → Security → Clipboard clear.

Are generated passwords truly random?

Yes — they are produced with your device’s cryptographically secure random number generator, the same class of randomness used by the encryption itself. Nothing is sent anywhere or derived from anything about you.

Security Tab

What does the Security tab do?

The Security tab analyses your whole vault and tells you what needs attention: an overall score, counts of weak and reused passwords, breach alerts, and the option to upgrade logins to passkeys. The “Needs attention” list shows each weak or reused password — with a reason — and Fix buttons that open the entry directly. Breached passwords are flagged separately, with a warning at the top of the entry’s detail view.

How is the security score calculated?

The ring shows a score out of 100 based on the health of your passwords — it is reduced by weak passwords and reused passwords, and fixing the flagged entries raises it. Passwords found in breaches are flagged separately under Breach alerts and don’t change the score. The ring stays visible even at a perfect 100/100, so you can always see your standing at a glance.

What counts as a “weak” password?

Passwords that would be easy to guess: too short, made of common words or patterns, or lacking variety. The fix is to open the entry, replace the password with a generated one, and update it on the website too.

Why is reusing passwords dangerous?

If one website leaks a password you’ve reused, attackers try that same password on your other accounts (“credential stuffing”). KeyVault flags every password used in more than one place so you can give each account its own generated password.

How do Breach alerts work? Is my password sent anywhere?

Tap Breach alerts to check your passwords against known data breaches via Have I Been Pwned. It uses k-anonymity: your password is hashed on your device and only the first 5 characters of the hash are sent — your password and its full hash never leave your phone, and the service cannot identify you. The check needs an internet connection. Note that the service reports how many times a password has appeared in breaches, but by design it cannot say which company leaked it.

What is “Upgrade to passkey”?

It lists your saved logins for websites that support passkeys, so you can replace passwords with a stronger, phishing-resistant sign-in. Tap a site to open its detail screen with step-by-step guidance, then tap Open site to create the passkey on the website in your browser.

Why does “Upgrade to passkey” show a note about the list’s date?

The list of which websites support passkeys is bundled with the app and labelled with the date it was last verified (“Passkey support data as of…”). Websites add passkey support over time, so the list is refreshed with app updates. If a listed site has changed its process, the site’s own instructions win.

What happens after I create a passkey on the website?

Come back to KeyVault and unlock it. KeyVault then checks whether a new passkey was saved for that site: if yes, you’ll see “Passkey saved”. If not, it asks “Did you create a passkey?” — tap Yes if you did (for example, if you saved it in another app) or No to leave the login unchanged. The check expires after about an hour, so a forgotten upgrade never pops up confusingly later.

What does “Keep password as backup” mean?

After upgrading a login to a passkey, you can keep the saved password as a fallback or remove it. Turning the option off first shows a warning: “Before turning off backup, ensure that the passkey is registered in KeyVault or another app.” Choose Cancel to keep things as they are, or Continue if you’re sure the passkey works — otherwise you could lock yourself out of that account.

Settings — Security

How do I change my master password?

Go to Settings → Security → Change password. You’ll confirm your current password and set a new one; your vault is re-encrypted with it. Your backups made under the old password still need the old password to open, so make a fresh backup afterwards.

What is Emergency access?

Emergency access creates a recovery kit: a 256-bit recovery key shown once, as a 58-character code and a QR code. Save it somewhere safe and private (printed paper in a secure place is ideal). If you ever forget your master password, the kit is the only way back in — using it starts a one-time session that requires you to set a new master password. The kit is powerful: anyone who has it and your vault can get in, so guard it like the passwords themselves. A copy of the recovery wrap travels inside your backups so a restored backup can still be recovered with the same kit.

How does the Fingerprint setting work?

Turning it on goes through Android’s own fingerprint enrollment, and your vault key is then protected by the Android Keystore. Turning it off removes that biometric unlock, and you’ll unlock with your master password instead.

What does “Clipboard clear” do?

When you copy a password, it sits in the clipboard where other apps could read it. This setting chooses how many seconds KeyVault waits before automatically clearing the clipboard after any copy. Shorter is safer; the value you pick is saved and applies to every copy action in the app.

What does “Password health” open?

It takes you straight to the Security tab, where your score, weak and reused password counts, and breach alerts live.

What does the Autofill setting do?

It shows whether KeyVault is currently set as your device’s Autofill service. Tapping it opens Android’s settings so you can enable KeyVault as the Autofill provider — after that, Android can offer your saved logins in other apps and browsers. Filling always happens on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.

Settings — Backup on This Phone

What does “Export backup” do?

It writes an encrypted backup file of your entire vault that you can save wherever you like. The file is protected by your master password — the same encryption as the vault itself; there is no separate backup password, and you’ll need your master password to import it later. Store the file somewhere safe; without your master password it cannot be opened.

What does “Auto backup” do?

Auto backup automatically keeps a local backup of your vault on your phone (in the Download/KeyVault folder) on the schedule you pick — Off, Daily, or Weekly. If your vault data is ever lost from the app, KeyVault can automatically restore from this backup. It’s a safety net for your device itself — it does not protect against losing the phone, so pair it with an exported or Drive backup.

How do I import a backup?

Settings → Backup → Import backup. There is no file to pick: KeyVault restores the most recent backup in your Download/KeyVault folder automatically. Just enter the master password that backup was made with, and KeyVault decrypts and restores it on your device.

How do I import my passwords from Google Chrome?

First export your passwords from Chrome as a CSV file (Chrome → Settings → Password Manager → Export). Then in KeyVault go to Settings → Backup → Chrome import, pick the CSV file, and KeyVault imports the entries, skipping duplicates you already have. The CSV file is not encrypted — delete it from your phone once the import is done.

What is the version line at the bottom of Settings?

It shows the exact KeyVault version installed on your phone (for example, “KeyVault 2.2.3”) — useful when checking whether you have the latest update.

Settings — Google Drive

How does Google Drive backup work?

If you link your Google account, KeyVault can keep an encrypted copy of your vault in your Drive’s hidden app-data folder (the drive.appdata scope — KeyVault cannot see any of your other Drive files). The vault is encrypted on your phone before upload, so the backup on Drive is unreadable to Google and to us; only your master password opens it.

How do I link or unlink Google Drive?

Settings → Google Drive → Link. Linking needs an internet connection. Sign in with Google’s own flow. To unlink, tap Unlink in the same place; unlinking stops all Drive activity but does not delete a backup already stored in Drive (delete that from your Google Drive settings if you want it gone).

What do “Back up now”, “Restore”, and “Sync now” each do?

Back up now uploads your current encrypted vault to Drive. Restore downloads the Drive backup and replaces your local vault with it. Sync now merges both ways so the newest version of each entry wins — use it when you use KeyVault on more than one phone. Each one needs your phone to be connected to the internet.

Is my Drive backup safe if someone accesses my Google account?

The backup file itself is encrypted with your vault’s encryption, so having the file is not enough — they would also need your master password. Even so, keep your Google account secured with a strong password and two-step verification.

Settings — Danger Zone

What does “Erase everything” do?

It permanently deletes your vault from this phone. Because this cannot be undone, it asks twice — the button changes to “Tap again to confirm” before anything is deleted. Erasing removes the local vault; if you kept backups (an exported file, the auto-backup folder, or Google Drive), those copies still exist until you delete them separately. Erase is the right step before selling or giving away your phone — after also deleting your backups.

Passkeys

What is a passkey, and why is it better than a password?

A passkey is a cryptographic key pair created for one website. The website keeps the public half; the private half stays in your vault and never leaves your device. Signing in proves you hold the private key — there’s nothing to type, nothing to phish, and nothing a data breach at the website can leak about you. You confirm sign-ins with your fingerprint or device unlock.

What do I need to use passkeys with KeyVault?

Android 14 or later, and KeyVault selected as your passkey provider in Android’s settings (Passwords, passkeys & autofill). Creating or using a passkey always requires unlocking KeyVault first.

How do I sign in with a passkey KeyVault holds?

On the website or app’s sign-in screen, choose the passkey option. Android shows its credential picker; pick the KeyVault entry, unlock, and you’re in — no password typed anywhere.

Can I add a note or a file to a passkey I’ve already saved?

Yes — without editing the passkey itself (a passkey can’t be edited). Open the passkey’s detail view and use the Add note / Edit note and Attach file actions there. No full edit is needed. The note and files are stored encrypted in your vault, tied to that passkey, and they travel with your encrypted backups and sync like everything else.

If I clear KeyVault from my recent apps mid-upgrade, is my passkey upgrade lost?

No. The pending upgrade is saved on your phone, not just in memory. Reopen KeyVault, unlock, and it picks up where you left off — checking for the new passkey and showing “Passkey saved” or asking whether you created one. Only the on-screen position resets, and the check quietly expires after an hour.

Troubleshooting

KeyVault shows the lock screen every time I return to it. Is that normal?

Yes — that’s the design. KeyVault always starts locked, and backgrounding the app clears decrypted data from memory. Unlock with your fingerprint or master password and everything is exactly where you left it — your last tab is even restored.

Linking Google Drive or running a breach check fails immediately. Why?

These features need the internet, so check that your phone is connected and try again. If you’re not connected, the feature itself will tell you it needs the internet.

A website doesn’t offer me passkey sign-in even though I saved one.

Check that KeyVault is selected as the passkey provider in Android’s settings and that you’re on Android 14 or later. Some websites only offer passkeys in certain browsers or regions — in that case, keep the password as backup (that’s exactly what the option is for).

I got a new phone. How do I move my vault?

On the old phone, either export a backup file or back up to Google Drive. On the new phone, install KeyVault, then import the backup file during setup or from Settings → Backup, or link Drive and use Restore/Sync. Enter your master password and everything — including passkey records and your emergency-access recovery wrap — comes across.

I erased my vault by accident. Can I get it back?

Only from a backup. If Auto backup was on, KeyVault may be able to restore automatically from the Download/KeyVault folder. Otherwise, import an exported backup file or restore from Google Drive if you linked it. Without any backup, erased data is gone — that permanence is what makes “Erase everything” a real protection.
